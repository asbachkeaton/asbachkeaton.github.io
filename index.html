Senior project

This capstone project explored whether Topological Data Analysis (TDA) could be used to identify patterns in complex data and improve predictions of professional Counter-Strike team performance. I applied TDA through persistence barcodes, analyzing the five-player statistical data of the top Counter-Strike teams from 2023 and 2024 and comparing their mathematical similarity. I calculated distances between persistence intervals to determine which 2024 team most closely resembled the 2023 Team of the Year, while also considering factors such as tournament wins, prize money, and world rankings.

Summary

Through this process, I found that TDA could reveal useful structural similarities in the data, but that relying on it alone was insufficient for making a reliable prediction. Ultimately, I learned that TDA is most effective as a tool for translating complex, multidimensional data into meaningful insights that can be combined with traditional analysis to support better decisions.

Problem

I set out to determine whether Topological Data Analysis (TDA) could identify meaningful similarities between professional Counter-Strike teams and help predict the best team of 2024. I used persistence barcodes, a mathematical way of representing the underlying structure of complex data, to compare player statistics across top teams. My goal was to determine whether TDA could provide useful predictive insights alongside traditional measures of team success.

Approach

I used Topological Data Analysis, specifically persistent homology and persistence barcodes, to analyze five-player statistical data from top Counter-Strike teams. I measured mathematical distances between the persistence intervals of teams to quantify their similarity, then compared those results with tournament wins, prize money, and world rankings to evaluate the prediction. I validated the analysis by comparing multiple teams against the 2023 Team of the Year and using these additional performance measures to assess whether the TDA results supported a reasonable conclusion.

Outcome

The analysis showed that Team Vitality’s 2024 data was most structurally similar to its 2023 data, while broader performance measures pointed toward Natus Vincere and G2 as the strongest teams. The main insight was that TDA can reveal useful patterns in complex data, but it should be combined with other information rather than used as a standalone prediction method. A major limitation was that the analysis did not account for factors such as tournament placements, prize money, and time spent ranked highly. A logical next step would be to analyze a larger dataset across multiple years and incorporate these additional performance measures to test whether TDA produces more reliable predictions.
